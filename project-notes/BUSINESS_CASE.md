## 1. The idea

- **Customer:** Who exactly is it for?
CampusSkillSwap is for StFX students who have skills they can teach and students who want to learn new skills but cannot afford paid lessons.

- **Problem:** What problem do they have today, and how do they solve it now?
Some students want to learn new skills but cannot afford paid tutoring. Others have useful skills but do not have an easy way to teach other students or get something in return. Students may also find it difficult to connect with people who can teach the skills they want to learn.

- **Solution:** What does your product do differently or better?
CampusSkillSwap allows students to exchange skills using Skill Credits instead of money. Students earn one credit when they complete a one-hour teaching session. They can then use that credit to learn a skill from another student. New students also receive one starter credit. This allows students to teach and learn without paying for lessons.

- **Revenue:** How does the company make money?
CampusSkillSwap would allow students to use the platform for free. The company could make money by charging universities an annual subscription fee to provide the platform to their students. This would allow students to exchange skills without paying money. StFX could be the first university to use the platform, with plans to expand to other universities in the future.

## 2. Where IT creates value

Which parts of the value chain does your product change, and how? Which course concepts apply (e.g. scale, distribution channels, differentiation, data, switching costs)?

CampusSkillSwap uses IT to make it easier for students to find each other and exchange skills. Instead of arranging lessons manually, students can use the platform to search for skills, book sessions, post their own skills, and track their credits. This changes how the service is delivered and makes the process easier to organize.
scale
CampusSkillSwap can grow without having to hire teachers because students provide the teaching themselves. The platform uses technology to connect students who have skills with those who want to learn. As more students join, the platform can offer more skills and learning opportunities without greatly increasing the cost of hiring staff.

Distribution channels
CampusSkillSwap can reach students through different channels, such as university emails, student societies, campus posters, and word of mouth. These channels can help the platform reach StFX students without spending too much on advertising. Student clubs and societies can also help introduce the platform to more students.

Differentiation
CampusSkillSwap is different from ordinary tutoring platforms because students do not need to pay money to learn. Instead, they earn Skill Credits by teaching other students and use those credits to learn new skills. They can also teach one student and use their credits to learn from someone else. This makes the platform more flexible and affordable.

Data
CampusSkillSwap can use data from platform activity to understand what students want to learn and which skills are available. For example, it can track popular skills, requested skills, and the number of sessions booked and completed. This information can help the business identify areas where more teachers are needed and improve the platform. It should only collect the information it needs and protect students' privacy.

Switching costs
Students may have reasons to stay with CampusSkillSwap after using it for some time. They may have earned credits, built a history of completed sessions, received reviews, and made connections with other students. These things could make it less attractive to move to another platform. This is called switching costs. However, these benefits would develop only after real students start using the platform.


## 3. Network effects


Does your product get more valuable as more people use it?
Yes. CampusSkillSwap becomes more valuable as more students join because it creates more opportunities for students to teach and learn.

- Is it a **one-sided** or **two-sided** market? Who are the sides?
CampusSkillSwap is a two-sided marketplace because it connects students who want to teach with students who want to learn. A student can also be both a teacher and a learner. For example, one student may teach Excel to earn a credit and use that credit to learn photography from another student.

- Where does the extra value come from as the network grows?
As more students join CampusSkillSwap, the platform becomes more useful. More teachers give learners more skills to choose from, while more learners create more opportunities for teachers to earn credits. Each new student can add value to the platform. This is called a network effect.

- How would you solve the "empty network" problem at launch?
To avoid starting with an empty platform, CampusSkillSwap could first focus on a small group of 20 to 30 interested StFX students. I would reach them through student societies, clubs, campus posters, and word of mouth. I would also encourage students to offer different skills, such as Excel, photography, programming, and public speaking. New students would receive one starter credit to encourage them to try the platform. Starting with StFX would help build an active community before expanding to other universities.


- Point to the part of your prototype that shows this.
My prototype shows this idea through the Explore page, which displays sample skills students can learn. The Post a Skill page allows students to add skills they can teach, while the Skill Credits system shows how students can earn and spend credits. These features demonstrate how students could connect as teachers and learners. The listings are fictional examples, not real student activity.

## 4. Can it be copied? (the moat question)

Anyone in this class could rebuild your website in a weekend with the same AI tool. So the code is **not** your competitive advantage.

- What about your business is **valuable, rare, hard to imitate and well-organized** (VRIN)?
The main advantage of CampusSkillSwap would not be its website because another person could build a similar website using AI. Its value would come from the community it builds among StFX students. It is valuable because it gives students an affordable way to teach and learn skills. A large and active community of StFX students could become rare because it would take time to attract enough students on both sides of the platform. It would also be difficult to imitate because a competitor could copy the website but would not immediately have the same student relationships, trust, and history of completed sessions. To organize and protect this advantage, the business would need student verification, a fair credit system, reviews, and a way to report problems. Other options, such as YouTube, paid tutoring, and study groups, could still meet some of the same needs, so CampusSkillSwap would need to offer a useful combination of peer learning and skill exchange.

- What would a competitor with a bigger budget need to copy you, and how long would it take?
A competitor with a bigger budget could easily pay developers to build a similar website and spend money on advertising to attract students. However, building the website would be easier than creating an active student community. The competitor would need to attract enough teachers and learners, build trust, collect genuine reviews, and develop relationships with universities. These things would take time and would depend on how well the platform serves students. Having more money could help a competitor grow faster, but it would not guarantee that students would trust and continue using the platform. I think building a trusted and active student community could take several months or longer, depending on how quickly students join and begin using the platform.

- Which of your advantages get *stronger* over time (data, network, brand, switching costs)?
Some advantages of CampusSkillSwap could become stronger as more students use the platform. First, the network would grow as more students join, creating more teaching and learning opportunities. Second, trust and reputation could improve through completed sessions and genuine student reviews. Third, the platform could collect useful data about popular skills and what students want to learn. Finally, students may become more likely to stay because they have earned credits, built a history of completed sessions, and made connections with other students. These advantages would develop over time as the platform gains real users.

## 5. Cloud and operations

What would it take to run this for real? Which parts would you buy as cloud services (SaaS, PaaS, IaaS), and what are the risks of relying on them?
To run CampusSkillSwap as a real business, it would need a secure system where students can create accounts, verify that they attend StFX, post skills, book sessions, and manage their Skill Credits. It would also need a database to store student profiles, skill listings, bookings, and session history. The business would need to maintain the platform, help users with problems, and make sure students can use it safely.

CampusSkillSwap could use Platform as a Service (PaaS) to host the website and run its backend without having to manage physical servers. It could also use Software as a Service (SaaS) for ready-made tools such as email notifications and customer support. Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS) could be useful in the future if the business needs more control over its servers and storage. Starting with PaaS and SaaS would make it easier to manage the platform without needing a large technical team.

Relying on cloud service providers also has some risks. If a provider experiences an outage, students may not be able to access the platform or book sessions. There may also be unexpected costs as the number of users grows. Another risk is vendor lock-in, which can make it difficult or expensive to move the platform to another provider. To reduce these risks, CampusSkillSwap would need regular backups, service monitoring, cost controls, and a plan for moving its data if necessary.
## 6. Risks and ethics

What could go wrong — for your customers, their data, or society? What would you do about it?
One risk is that students' personal information could be exposed or stolen. CampusSkillSwap would collect information such as student names, StFX email addresses, skill listings, and booking history. To protect students, the platform should only collect the information it needs. It should also use secure passwords, encryption, access controls, and regular security updates to prevent unauthorized access.

Another risk is that people who are not StFX students could create fake accounts or provide false information about their skills. This could reduce trust in the platform. To prevent this, CampusSkillSwap should require students to verify their accounts using their StFX email addresses. It should also allow users to report suspicious accounts and false skill listings.

There is also a risk that students may experience harassment or feel unsafe during a skill exchange. To reduce this risk, CampusSkillSwap should encourage students to meet in public places on campus. The platform should also have options for users to report or block other students. A clear code of conduct would help users understand the expected behaviour, and reports should be reviewed properly.

Another risk is that students may try to earn credits without completing teaching sessions or may falsely claim that a session was completed. To prevent this, both the teacher and learner should confirm that a session took place before credits are awarded. The platform should also keep a record of completed sessions and provide a way to report disagreements. This would help make the credit system fair for everyone.
